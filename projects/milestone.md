**Worth 10 of the project's 50 points. One submission per team** (any member submits for the team).

## What to submit, and when

- **One PDF of your slides**, submitted to Brightspace → Assignments → *Project milestone —
  presentation and running artifact*, **by 11:00 a.m. on Tuesday, November 17**, before the talks.

## The talk: about 7 minutes, then one question

All eight teams present in class on November 17, in team order A–H. Plan for **7 minutes**, then one
question from the room. Talks are held to time so that every team gets its slot.

## Required in the talk

1. **A live demo of your tool running.** Your verifier, or the formal-methods tool your project
   depends on, runs on real input and prints real output in front of the class. A plan does not
   count, and neither does a trained model with nothing checking it. Bring a short screen recording
   as a fallback in case the live run fails. **This is the gate: 3 of the 10 points.**
2. **Progress since the proposal, on both halves**: the formal-methods half and the AI half. If you
   changed direction, say what changed and why.
3. **Mathematical formulation.** Every technical term you use (a property, a metric, robustness) is
   defined mathematically on a slide.
4. **Presentation basics.** Team members' names on the title slide; figures readable from the back of
   the room, with consistent axes, fonts and units.
5. **Repository.** Accessible to the instructor, with real commit history and a README that says how
   to run your tool.

**Undergraduate teams:** also show a draft of your CS Immersion Showcase poster, or one slide with
its layout, so it can get feedback. The poster file itself is due **Thursday, December 3**.

## How it is graded — 10 points

| Criterion | Points | What full marks looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Gate: a verifier runs** | 3 | Your tool executes and produces real output on real input, shown live (a recording only as a fallback). |
| Progress since proposal | 2 | Substantive movement on both halves. Changes of direction are fine, and are explained. |
| Technical depth | 2 | You can answer questions about *why* the tool says what it says, not just what it printed. |
| Mathematical formulation | 1 | Every technical term you use is defined mathematically on a slide. |
| Presentation quality | 1 | Names on the title slide. Consistent axes, fonts and units. No slide too dense to read from the back. |
| Repository and process | 1 | Accessible repo, real commit history across the semester, a README that explains how to run it. |

A team that does not clear the gate cannot earn full marks on the final either: by mid-November there
is not enough time left to do the remaining work properly.
